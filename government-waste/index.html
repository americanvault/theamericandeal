NSRA Title XXXV · Title XXXIX · Payment Integrity

Stop Paying Out Money We Shouldn't, and Spend What Congress Passed

The Government Accountability Office estimated federal improper payments at about $186 billion in fiscal year 2025. The NSRA treats that as a fiscal emergency, not a rounding error: every large program must measure its errors, a permanent analytics center stops bad payments before they go out, and the old computer systems that cause many of them get replaced on a deadline.

What it does

ProvisionDraft text
Measure every big programEvery program paying out more than $100 million a year must publish an annual improper-payment estimate (Sec. 3509(a))
Stop it before it goes outA permanent payment-integrity analytics center open to every agency and Inspector General (Sec. 3509(b)); Treasury's Do Not Pay system expanded to real-time verification, including full death records (Sec. 3904)
Apply onceOne application screens a household for Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP and health coverage; each program still decides under its own rules, and States choose whether to use it (Sec. 3907)
Catch duplicate paymentsOne shared check for identity, deaths, income and people enrolled in two States' Medicaid or in two kinds of coverage at once, with a log of every lookup, no master database, and a person reviewing every case before benefits change (Sec. 3907(d)–(f))
Fix the leaky systemsDecades-old benefit, tax, and payment systems retired on a seven-year schedule, paid first from the savings (Secs. 3902, 3905)
Spend what Congress passedNo "pocket rescissions" in the last 45 days of a fiscal year; withheld funds stay available; expedited GAO lawsuits; States and localities can sue to release funds owed to them (Sec. 3507)
Public apportionmentsOMB must post every apportionment, with footnotes, within two business days (Sec. 3507(f))
Independent watchdogsAn Inspector General removed without the required notice stays in office with full pay until the notice is given; no cutting off an IG's records or budget to stop an audit (Sec. 3508)

Why both parties should want it

Fiscal conservatives get the largest documented pool of avoidable federal spending. Good-government advocates get transparency and independent watchdogs. Neither side has to agree on the size of government to agree that money should go to the right person, in the right amount, for the purpose Congress set. The impoundment rules apply to every President equally; a sponsor can set them to take effect at the start of the next term so no current officeholder is targeted.

Operative text: Title XXXV (Secs. 3507–3510) and Title XXXIX (Secs. 3901–3906).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money is lost to improper payments?

The Government Accountability Office estimated about $186 billion in federal improper payments in fiscal year 2025. Not all improper payments are fraud; many are payments made without required documentation, to the wrong amount, or to ineligible recipients.

What is a pocket rescission?

It is when the executive branch proposes canceling funds so late in the fiscal year that the money expires before Congress can act, effectively canceling spending without a vote. Section 3507 requires such funds to be released in time to be spent.

Does this cost money?

The systems work is paid first from the improper-payment savings it produces, and any shortfall comes from capped, deficit-neutral SIRF funds certified by OMB.

Can it pass on its own?

Yes. These sections are self-contained and can be introduced as a standalone payment-integrity and budget-honesty bill.
