The plan

Five pillars. One American Deal.

The NSRA is one connected program: lower costs for households, a stronger start for families, an economy that makes things here, a government that answers to voters, and a country that pays its way. Every title and every section is in the full bill.

Lower the bills every family pays.

A $0-deductible plan for the 30 million uninsured, with no premium up to about $80,000 for a family of four, through private insurers; G7 prices for medicines built on taxpayer-funded research; $35 insulin.

Land, water and air

Clean water, safe food, clean energy, wild places

A country that catches up has to be one people want to live in. The NSRA funds the basics every family counts on, and makes polluters, not taxpayers, pay to clean up.

Secs. 1002, 1004

Clean water for every home

Safe drinking water is guaranteed at no more than 1% of household income, with $15B a year to rebuild water systems and fix the ones that fail federal standards.

Secs. 704–706

Safe food, healthy soil

Additives banned by three or more G7 nations are banned here too, old “safe” designations get reviewed, and $20B a year builds regional food systems and helps farmers move off harmful chemicals.

Secs. 901, 904, 905

Cleaner, cheaper power

Big data centers must bring their own clean power and send the extra to the grid, credited to household bills, while $25B a year modernizes the national grid.

Sec. 1005

Bring wild places back

$22B a year for wildlife crossings, habitat corridors, restored public lands and watersheds, urban trees, and pollinator habitat.

Secs. 1101–1104

Polluters pay to clean up

Industrial sites post a bond worth 150% of the cleanup cost up front, so taxpayers never get stuck with the bill, and contamination is cleaned at the source.

Secs. 1703, 1704

Power from dams we already have

Add clean hydropower to existing dams instead of building new ones, with licensing on a fixed schedule.

How it passes

Five acts. Each one pays its own way.

The American Deal is 40 titles organized into five coordinated acts. Each act is self-funding and can pass on its own, so if some stall, Americans still win. Together they cut the deficit by about $5.0 trillion over ten years and write a balanced-budget path into law.

Act I

The American Fiscal Integrity Act

Closes the offshore tax gap, collects taxes already owed, stops Pentagon waste, and writes the balanced-budget path into law. It is the plan’s largest source of revenue.

Ways & Means · Finance · Armed Services
Act II

The American Health Security Act

$0-deductible, $0-deductible baseline coverage through private insurers, G7 drug prices, $35 insulin, and dental, vision and hearing care for every child.

Energy & Commerce · Finance · Veterans’ Affairs
Act III

The American Economic Security Act

Homes for families instead of funds, zero-interest student loans, $1,000 at birth for every child, paid leave, and science back in front.

Banking · Judiciary · Education · HELP
Act IV

The American Supply Chain Security Act

Grow, build and power America here: food security, cheaper energy, open broadband, and critical supply chains brought home.

Agriculture · Energy & Commerce · Transportation
Act V

The American Accountability and Data Rights Act

An independent watchdog against fraud and market rigging, real rights over your personal data, and government that works online.

Judiciary · Commerce · EPW

Balance the budget

A balanced-budget path, written into law

Under current law, CBO projects deficits of about 6% of the economy, rising from $1.9 trillion to $3.1 trillion. The NSRA pays for every program it creates and still cuts the deficit by about $5.0 trillion over ten years. Then it writes hard deficit limits into law, enforced automatically if Congress misses them.

Year 4

Exempt

Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans, SNAP and the Basic Minimum Plan are exempt from any automatic cut, and the automatic tax lever never touches households under $400,000.

$305B

Questions people ask

The American Deal, answered

What is the American Deal (NSRA)?

The National Sovereignty and Resilience Act (NSRA), called the American Deal, is a complete federal bill of 40 titles and 261 sections written by Santonio D. Sanders, an independent citizen author. It lowers household costs (health coverage, prescription drugs, housing, student loans), bans congressional stock trading, and writes deficit limits into law, while cutting the deficit by about $5.0 trillion over ten years.

Has the NSRA been introduced in Congress?

Not yet. The full text is published so any member of Congress or candidate can introduce all of it or any part of it. Six planks, including the congressional stock trading ban and the government-waste provisions, are written to move on their own in 2027.

Does the NSRA ban stock trading by members of Congress?

Yes. Title XXIX requires members of Congress, their spouses, senior executive officials and federal judges to divest individual stocks within 180 days, with penalties larger than any trade could earn.

Does the NSRA raise taxes on people earning under $400,000?

No income-tax increase applies to households under $400,000. The Social Security tax increase applies only to wages above $400,000. Two measures reach most households and are disclosed: a carbon fee, with energy-bill credits for lower-income households, and a paid-leave contribution of 0.4% of wages from workers and 0.4% from employers that funds 12 weeks of paid leave.

Does the NSRA cut Social Security or Medicare?

Nothing changes for anyone eligible before 2032, the retirement age does not change, and Medicare benefits, premiums and cost-sharing are not cut. To keep Social Security solvent, benefits grow more slowly for future retirees with the highest lifetime earnings, and the minimum benefit rises to 125% of the poverty line for 30-year workers. Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' benefits and SNAP are exempt from automatic budget cuts.

How does the NSRA balance the budget?

It cuts the deficit by about $5.0 trillion over ten years including lower interest costs (range $4.0 to $5.7 trillion), caps the deficit at 4.5% of GDP by Year 4 and 3% by Year 10, and requires Congress to vote by Year 8 on a plan to balance the budget by Year 15. If Congress misses a limit, an automatic trigger closes the gap, half from high-income tax breaks and half from non-exempt spending.

How is the NSRA paid for?

About $635 billion a year in revenue at full ramp from 19 sources, led by Social Security tax on wages above $400,000, a 28% corporate rate, a global minimum tax, IRS enforcement, a 0.1% financial transaction tax and a 39.6% top rate above $400,000. After about $446 billion in program costs and infrastructure, it reduces the deficit by about $189 billion a year, and its Fiscal Sustainability Plan adds about $305 billion a year at full effect. Estimates are checked with open-source models and published CBO, JCT and SSA estimates; official scoring is pending.

What does the NSRA do about prescription drug prices?

It caps insulin at $35 a month, ties U.S. drug prices to the median price in other G7 countries, and expands Medicare drug price negotiation to 50 drugs a year.

What health coverage does the NSRA provide?

The Basic Minimum Plan gives the roughly 30 million uninsured Americans coverage with no deductible. It has no premium up to 250% of the poverty line, and premiums are capped at 8.5% of income above that. It is run through the existing ACA marketplaces and pays providers at Medicare rates.

What does the NSRA do about housing costs?

It caps corporate ownership of single-family homes at 50 per metro area, phases out excess holdings gradually so local markets are not flooded, and rewards towns that allow more homes to be built.

Who wrote the NSRA?

Santonio D. Sanders, a U.S. Navy veteran, former VA ambulatory care director and former U.S. State Department official. The project is independent and is not affiliated with any party, PAC or elected official.
