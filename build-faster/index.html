NSRA Title XVII · Sec. 905 · Sec. 1710

America Can Build Again: Permits on a Clock, Costs in Public, Power from Dams We Already Have

The American Society of Civil Engineers grades U.S. infrastructure a C, with a $3.7 trillion ten-year funding gap. The country built about 888 miles of new high-voltage transmission in 2024, a fraction of what the grid needs. Title XVII applies lessons from Japan and China to build faster and cheaper, while keeping every substantive environmental, safety, labor, and tribal-consultation protection.

What it does

ProvisionDraft text
Power existing damsOnly a small share of America's roughly 90,000 dams generate electricity. A revolving fund adds generation at non-powered dams and modernizes old hydropower, with dam safety fixed first (Sec. 1703)
Licenses on a scheduleFERC publishes one schedule for every federal decision on a hydropower license; total extensions capped at one year (Sec. 1704)
Inspect everything every 5 yearsA Japan-style hands-on inspection of bridges, dams, and other covered assets at least every five years, with a public registry and required repairs (Sec. 1705)
Publish what it costsStandard unit costs (per lane-mile, per kilometer of track, per megawatt) reported and benchmarked; projects far above the median must explain why; a public library of pre-approved standard designs (Sec. 1706)
Permits on a clockOne federal schedule per project on the Permitting Dashboard, limits on deadline extensions, and a 150-day window for lawsuits (Sec. 1707)
Grid backboneA national high-voltage transmission backbone plan and 15% of grid funds for long-duration storage, including pumped hydro (Sec. 905)
Protect before disasterPre-disaster mitigation for wildfire, flood, and extreme heat restored and protected from administrative cancellation, with a rural set-aside (Sec. 1710)

Faster, not looser

Every section keeps substantive law intact: the Clean Water Act, Endangered Species Act, Federal Power Act conditions, State water-quality authority, labor standards, and tribal consultation. What changes is the clock and the transparency. Projects are funded inside existing allocations, with no new authorization fight.

Operative text: Title XVII (Secs. 1701–1710) and Sec. 905.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does this weaken environmental protections?

No. Sections 1703, 1704, and 1707 expressly preserve every substantive environmental, public health, safety, labor, civil rights, and tribal-consultation requirement. The changes are to schedules, transparency, and the window for lawsuits.

Why focus on existing dams?

Adding turbines to a dam that already exists avoids building a new reservoir. Only a small share of U.S. dams produce power today, and many need safety work anyway, so the program fixes safety first and adds clean power where it pays.

What does Japan do differently?

Since 2014 Japan has required a close, hands-on inspection of every road bridge and tunnel every five years, with results graded and published. Section 1705 adopts that standard.

Does it cost new money?

Title XVII is funded within existing Sec. 905 grid and Sec. 1004 water allocations of the bill, with a budget-neutrality certification (Sec. 1708).
