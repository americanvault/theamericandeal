A Real Congressional Stock Trading Ban, Not Another Disclosure Rule

Title XXIX of the National Sovereignty and Resilience Act is a full ban on individual stock trading by members of Congress, senior executive branch officials, and federal judges. It requires divestiture, penalties that cost more than the trade could earn, and citizen enforcement.

Why the STOCK Act was not enough

The 2012 STOCK Act requires disclosure but never banned the trades. Late filings are routine and the penalties are nominal. In 2026 the House passed a narrower bill that stops new purchases but lets members keep the stocks they already hold. Title XXIX closes that gap.

What Title XXIX does

ProvisionDraft text
Who is coveredMembers of Congress, Senate-confirmed and Executive Schedule I–V officials, federal judges and justices (Sec. 2902(a))
What is bannedTrading or holding individual equities, corporate bonds, commodity and currency contracts, related derivatives, and non-transparent private funds
Family membersOfficials may not direct a spouse, domestic partner, or minor child to make prohibited trades
Still allowedBroad-based index funds and ETFs, Treasury securities, fixed annuities and pensions, qualified blind trusts (Sec. 2902(b))
DivestitureWithin 180 days of enactment, or 90 days after taking office (Sec. 2903(a))
Civil penaltiesFull disgorgement, plus a penalty of twice the transaction value, an ethics referral, and public disclosure within 3 business days
Criminal penaltiesWillful violations prosecuted as securities fraud under 18 U.S.C. § 1348
Citizen enforcementPrivate right of action in federal district court with fee-shifting (Sec. 2903(d))
CostBudget-neutral; penalties fund an accountability account (Sec. 2904)

Built to stand alone

Title XXIX is self-contained. A member can introduce it as a stand-alone bill, offer it as an amendment, or campaign on it as part of the full 2028 cost-of-living agenda. Polling has consistently found broad public support across party lines for banning congressional stock trading.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the NSRA ban members of Congress from owning stock?

Yes. Title XXIX bars members of Congress, Senate-confirmed and Executive Schedule officials, and federal judges from buying, selling, or holding individual stocks, bonds, commodity contracts, or related derivatives while in office. Broad index funds, Treasury securities, and qualified blind trusts remain allowed.

Does it cover spouses and children?

Yes. Covered officials may not direct or advise a spouse, domestic partner, or minor child to make a prohibited trade. Doing so willfully is treated as securities fraud.

How is this different from the bill the House passed in 2026?

The House-passed Stop Insider Trading Act bars new purchases but lets members keep the stocks they already own, subject to advance notice of sales, and it does not reach the executive branch. NSRA Title XXIX requires full divestiture within 180 days and also covers senior executive officials and federal judges.

What are the penalties?

Disgorgement of all profits, a civil penalty of twice the transaction value, an ethics referral, public disclosure on the congressional record within three business days, and criminal prosecution for willful violations. Any citizen may sue to enforce it, with attorneys' fees for a prevailing plaintiff.
