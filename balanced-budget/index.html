NSRA Sec. 3406(f) · Balanced Budget Path · Act I

A Balanced-Budget Path, Written Into Law

CBO projects federal deficits of about 6% of the economy, rising from $1.9 trillion in 2026 to $3.1 trillion by 2036. With its Fiscal Sustainability Plan, the NSRA pays for every program it creates and a middle-class income-tax cut, and still cuts the deficit by a modeled $3.2 trillion over ten years, including lower interest costs. Then it writes hard deficit limits into law and enforces them automatically.

The limits

DeadlineLimit in law
Year 4Deficit at or below 4.5% of GDP, down from about 6% (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(A))
Year 8Deficit at or below 3% of GDP by the end of two presidential terms, the level at which the debt stops growing faster than the economy (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(B)–(C))
Every year in betweenA straight-line glide path published by CBO, so progress is measured every year (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(D))

How it is enforced

StepWhat happens
August 15CBO reports whether next year’s deficit, under current law, misses the limit, and by how much
Congress acts firstA Fiscal Target Act that closes the gap gets a guaranteed vote within 30 days of session
October 1 triggerIf Congress does not act, the gap closes automatically: half by capping tax breaks for incomes over $400,000 (single) or $450,000 (joint), half by an across-the-board cut split evenly between defense and non-defense
Speed limitNew automatic adjustments are capped at 0.5% of GDP a year, so there is no sudden shock to the economy
Recession or warEnforcement pauses and resumes afterward; deadlines move only by the length of the pause

The Fiscal Sustainability Plan (Sec. 3410)

MeasureWhat it does
Hospital price limitPrivate insurers pay hospitals no more than twice what Medicare pays for the same service (three times for rural and critical-access hospitals). Families and employers pay lower premiums, and higher take-home pay raises federal revenue.
Medicare drug negotiation50 drugs negotiated a year instead of up to 20, plus one payment system for post-acute care. No benefit, premium or cost-sharing changes.
Spending capsDefense and non-defense appropriations grow at inflation minus 1.5% for ten years. Veterans’ medical care is held at inflation.
High-end tax breaksAbove $400,000 of income, deductions and exclusions are worth no more than 28 cents on the dollar.
Carbon feeRises $5 a ton a year to $75, with half returned to households up to 400% of poverty as energy bill credits.
WasteEvery large program must cut its improper-payment rate by a third within three years.
Crop insuranceTaxpayers cover 40% of premiums on average instead of about 60%, with full support kept for beginning, veteran and smaller farmers; insurer overhead payments are capped. These savings, about $5 billion a year, go to farmers switching away from banned pesticides (Sec. 705(d)) and are not counted in the total below.

Together these save about $345 billion a year at full effect. With the core programs, the bill cuts the deficit by a modeled $3.2 trillion over ten years including lower interest costs (range $2.3–3.8 trillion). An American Resource Fund (Sec. 3411) also stops spending new oil, gas and offshore-wind royalties as they come in and builds a permanent fund, the way Alaska has since 1976, with half its earnings going to Social Security after Year 10.

Who is protected

No automatic cut can touch Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, the Basic Minimum Plan, veterans’ compensation and health care, SNAP, the $1,000-at-birth deposits, low-income tax credits, or military pay. The automatic tax lever applies only above the $400,000 / $450,000 income limits.

The honest math

The core programs and the middle-class tax cut pay for themselves at full ramp, and the Fiscal Sustainability Plan of Sec. 3410 supplies the deficit reduction. Our model puts the deficit at about 5.4% of GDP in Year 4 and 5.2% in Year 8, down from about 6% under current law. Reaching the Year 4 limit of 4.5% takes about 0.8% of GDP more, and the Year 8 limit of 3% about 2.1% more, from a plan Congress passes or, failing that, the automatic trigger. That is a large lift, which is why the law enforces it rather than leaving it to good intentions. A fully balanced budget within two terms is not possible without cutting Social Security, Medicare or Medicaid or raising taxes on the middle class, so the bill does not promise one. It promises a deficit low enough that the debt stops growing faster than the economy.

Operative text: Sec. 3406(f) (Title XXXIV). Severable and able to move on its own (Sec. 3406(f)(9)). Dollar figures are NSRA model projections (version 2026.10) on CBO’s February 2026 baseline; CBO has not scored the bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the NSRA balance the budget?

It cuts the deficit by a modeled $3.2 trillion over ten years and writes hard limits into law for two presidential terms: a deficit at or below 4.5% of GDP by Year 4 and 3% by Year 8, enforced automatically. At 3% the debt stops growing faster than the economy. A fully balanced budget within eight years would require cutting Social Security, Medicare or Medicaid or raising middle-class taxes, so the bill does not promise one.

Will this cut Social Security or Medicare?

Not for anyone retired or eligible before 2032, and never through the automatic trigger: Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' benefits, SNAP, the Basic Minimum Plan and military pay are exempt under Sec. 3406(f)(5). The bill makes no change to Social Security benefits; the Social Security payroll tax on wages above $400,000 (Sec. 2408) goes to the Social Security trust funds.

Will it raise my taxes?

Not through the automatic trigger: its only tax lever is a cap on the value of tax breaks for incomes over $400,000 (single) or $450,000 (joint). Other parts of the NSRA, such as the paid-leave contribution and the carbon fee, apply more broadly and are scored in the fiscal model; lower-income households receive energy bill credits that offset the carbon fee.

What stops a future Congress from ignoring it?

Nothing can stop a future Congress from changing a law, but changing this one requires a public vote to abandon the limits. The limits, any shortfall, and every adjustment are reported quarterly.

Can it pass on its own?

Yes. Sec. 3406(f)(9) makes it severable and allows it to be introduced and enacted as a separate bill.
