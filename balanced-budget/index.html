NSRA Sec. 3406(f) · Balanced Budget Path · Act I

A Balanced-Budget Path, Written Into Law

CBO projects federal deficits of about 6% of the economy, rising from $1.9 trillion in 2026 to $3.1 trillion by 2036. The NSRA pays for every program it creates and still cuts the deficit by a modeled $5.0 trillion over ten years, including lower interest costs. Then it writes hard deficit limits into law and enforces them automatically.

The limits

DeadlineLimit in law
Year 4Deficit at or below 4.5% of GDP, down from about 6% (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(A))
Year 10Deficit at or below 3% of GDP, the level that keeps the debt from growing faster than the economy (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(B))
Year 8A required vote on a Balanced Budget Act that balances the budget by Year 15 (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(C))
Every year in betweenA straight-line glide path published by CBO, so progress is measured every year (Sec. 3406(f)(2)(D))

How it is enforced

StepWhat happens
August 15CBO reports whether next year’s deficit, under current law, misses the limit, and by how much
Congress acts firstA Fiscal Target Act that closes the gap gets a guaranteed vote within 30 days of session
October 1 triggerIf Congress does not act, the gap closes automatically: half by capping tax breaks for incomes over $400,000 (single) or $450,000 (joint), half by an across-the-board cut split evenly between defense and non-defense
Speed limitNew automatic adjustments are capped at 0.5% of GDP a year, so there is no sudden shock to the economy
Recession or warEnforcement pauses and resumes afterward; deadlines move only by the length of the pause

The Fiscal Sustainability Plan (Sec. 3410)

MeasureWhat it does
Social Security solvencySlows benefit growth only for future retirees with the highest lifetime earnings and raises the minimum benefit to 125% of poverty for 30-year workers. No change for anyone eligible before 2032, no change to the retirement age or cost-of-living raises.
Medicare drug negotiation50 drugs negotiated a year instead of up to 20, plus one payment system for post-acute care. No benefit, premium or cost-sharing changes.
Spending capsDefense and non-defense appropriations grow at inflation minus 1% for ten years. Veterans’ medical care is held at inflation.
High-end tax breaksAbove $400,000 of income, deductions and exclusions are worth no more than 28 cents on the dollar.
Carbon feeRises $5 a ton a year to $75, with 30% still returned to lower-income households as energy bill credits.
WasteEvery large program must cut its improper-payment rate by a third within three years.
Crop insuranceTaxpayers cover 40% of premiums on average instead of about 60%, with full support kept for beginning, veteran and smaller farmers; insurer overhead payments are capped.

Together these save about $305 billion a year at full effect. With the core programs, the bill cuts the deficit by a modeled $5.0 trillion over ten years including lower interest costs (range $4.0–5.7 trillion). An American Resource Fund (Sec. 3411) also stops spending new oil, gas and offshore-wind royalties as they come in and builds a permanent fund, the way Alaska has since 1976, with half its earnings going to Social Security after Year 10.

Who is protected

No automatic cut can touch Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, the Basic Minimum Plan, veterans’ compensation and health care, SNAP, the $1,000-at-birth deposits, low-income tax credits, or military pay. The automatic tax lever applies only above the $400,000 / $450,000 income limits.

The honest math

The bill’s own savings, about $190 billion a year from the core programs, together with the Fiscal Sustainability Plan of Sec. 3410, cover about two-thirds of the drop to the Year 4 limit. Reaching the Year 10 limit takes about 2% of GDP more, from a plan Congress passes or, failing that, the automatic trigger. That is a large lift, comparable to the 1990 and 1993 deficit agreements combined, which is why the law forces a vote rather than leaving it to good intentions. A full balanced budget cannot come from the bill’s revenue alone while protecting Social Security and Medicare, so the bill requires Congress to vote on a plan to get there.

Operative text: Sec. 3406(f) (Title XXXIV), carried with Act I, the American Fiscal Integrity Act. Severable and able to move on its own (Sec. 3406(f)(9)). Dollar figures are NSRA model projections (version 2026.2) on CBO’s February 2026 baseline; CBO has not scored the bill.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the NSRA balance the budget?

It cuts the deficit by a modeled $5.0 trillion over ten years and writes hard limits into law: a deficit at or below 4.5% of GDP by Year 4 and 3% by Year 10, enforced automatically. It also requires Congress to vote by Year 8 on a plan that balances the budget by Year 15.

Will this cut Social Security or Medicare?

Not for anyone retired or eligible before 2032, and never through the automatic trigger: Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans' benefits, SNAP, the Basic Minimum Plan and military pay are exempt under Sec. 3406(f)(5). To keep Social Security solvent, Sec. 3410(b) slows benefit growth for future retirees with the highest lifetime earnings and raises the minimum benefit for low earners. The retirement age does not change.

Will it raise my taxes?

Not through the automatic trigger: its only tax lever is a cap on the value of tax breaks for incomes over $400,000 (single) or $450,000 (joint). Other parts of the NSRA, such as the paid-leave contribution and the carbon fee, apply more broadly and are scored in the fiscal model; lower-income households receive energy bill credits that offset the carbon fee.

What stops a future Congress from ignoring it?

Nothing can stop a future Congress from changing a law, but changing this one requires a public vote to abandon the limits. The limits, any shortfall, and every adjustment are reported quarterly.

Can it pass on its own?

Yes. Sec. 3406(f)(9) makes it severable and allows it to be introduced and enacted as a separate bill.
