NSRA Title XXXVII · Science, Research & Talent

Put America Back in the Lead on Science

Federal research spending was 1.86% of GDP in 1964. It is about 0.65% today. China now spends roughly as much as the U.S. on R&D at purchasing-power parity, and Congress has funded the National Science Foundation at about half of what it authorized in the CHIPS and Science Act. Title XXXVII fixes the gap on a schedule, with a hard cap and a deficit-neutral funding source.

What it does

ProvisionDraft text
A national targetFederal R&D reaches 1% of GDP within ten years, on a published straight-line path; at least 30% of the increase to basic research (Sec. 3702)
Fund what Congress already passedNSF, DOE Office of Science, and NIST at CHIPS and Science Act levels; NIH growing with biomedical inflation plus one point (Sec. 3703(a))
No more lab whiplashA Research Stability Reserve keeps existing multi-year grants at 90% or more through budget fights; half of new grants run four years or longer; 15% reserved for early-career scientists (Sec. 3703(b)–(d))
From lab to factoryRegional tech hubs outside the top ten research metros and lab-to-market grants with a domestic-manufacturing condition on resulting patents (Sec. 3704)
Research securityNo funds to participants in malign foreign talent programs or entities controlled by countries of concern; full disclosure of foreign support (Sec. 3705)
Keep the talent we trainGreen cards outside the annual caps for U.S.-earned STEM PhDs and master's graduates from research universities with a job offer at or above the median wage, with adversary-country screening and anti-displacement attestation (Sec. 3706)
CostCapped at $30 billion a year from SIRF Tier 3 capacity, certified deficit-neutral by OMB under the conservative CBO-style floor (Sec. 3707)

Operative text: Title XXXVII (Secs. 3701–3708).

Frequently Asked Questions

How far behind is U.S. science funding?

Federal R&D fell from about 1.86% of GDP in 1964 to roughly 0.6–0.7% today. Total U.S. R&D intensity, about 3.5% of GDP, trails Israel and South Korea, and China's spending is roughly equal to America's at purchasing-power parity.

Is this just more spending?

It is capped at $30 billion a year, draws only on deficit-neutral SIRF capacity certified by OMB, and mostly funds levels Congress already authorized in 2022.

Does the green card provision raise immigration overall?

It exempts a narrow group from numerical caps: people who earned an advanced STEM degree at a U.S. research university and have a qualifying job offer. It requires adversary-country screening and an attestation that no U.S. worker is displaced.

How does it protect research from China?

Section 3705 bars funding to participants in malign foreign talent recruitment programs and to entities controlled by countries of concern, and requires disclosure of all foreign research support, applied on the basis of documented affiliations, not nationality or ethnicity.
