What’s new in the American Deal

Changes to the bill, the numbers and the site, newest first. The full text always reflects the latest version.

November 8, 2026

Guaranteed help for farmers, regional food funded again, and a new Title XL

Farmers converting away from banned pesticides now have guaranteed funding (Sec. 705(d)), paid from the crop-insurance reform of Sec. 3410(h), about $5 billion a year, and the pesticide deadlines wait until that money is certified available. Regional food processing, cold storage and food hubs (Sec. 706) get $5 billion a year moved from the chips allocation, open to any qualified business, cooperative or nonprofit, with owners keeping control. New Title XL adds protection from scams, identity theft and elder exploitation, community safety grants, government neutrality among faiths, rights to contraception, IVF and abortion care, and a public, open-source digital voting system: every encrypted ballot is posted live to a public ledger, each voter can confirm from a phone that their ballot was counted, anyone can verify the totals, and paper ballots back it all up. Voting from personal devices starts with pilots and expands only after federal security certification and a vote of Congress. The programs still pay for themselves; ten-year deficit reduction is about $2.9 trillion including interest.

November 8, 2026

The programs pay for themselves again: $2.9 trillion in ten-year deficit reduction

The rewilding and regional food-system programs move from automatic funding to the normal annual budget process, and the Phase 2 wage-floor tax credit is withdrawn, saving about $50 billion a year. With that, the bill's programs and its middle-class tax cut are paid for by its own revenue, and the Fiscal Sustainability Plan supplies the deficit reduction: about $2.9 trillion over ten years. The modeled deficit is about 5.5% of GDP in Years 4 and 10.

November 8, 2026

A middle-class tax cut, and the 21%% corporate rate kept

The 12%% and 22%% income-tax brackets drop to 10%% and 20%% (new Sec. 2421), about $128 billion a year, recaptured from incomes above $400,000. The corporate rate stays at 21%%, the gross-receipts infrastructure fee is withdrawn, and energy-bill credits now reach households up to 400%% of the poverty line. Ten-year deficit reduction fell from $5.5 trillion to $2.3 trillion (superseded: see the update above), and the modeled deficit was about 5.6%% of GDP in Year 4 and 5.7%% in Year 10. In our economic model, households up to the 70th percentile of lifetime income now come out ahead, and debt falls about half as fast.

November 7, 2026

Every number now matches Schedule G

Each section that states a revenue estimate now states the same figure as Schedule G, and Schedule G has a crosswalk to Section 3402. Infrastructure allocations now add to exactly $95 billion, and rural health infrastructure is counted once. The ten-year tables are labeled as nominal dollars, and the modeled deficit is shown next to the legal limits: about 4.9%% of GDP in Year 4 against a 4.5%% limit, and 4.7%% in Year 10 against 3%%. A new table shows what each of the five acts costs and which revenue it needs to pass on its own. Title XXXIV, which holds the deficit limits and the Fiscal Sustainability Plan, now sits in Act I.

November 7, 2026

A lower corporate rate, fewer loopholes: $5.5 trillion in ten-year deficit reduction (superseded)

The corporate rate is now 25%%, not 28%%, and the difference is paid for by closing narrow breaks: the export-income deduction, LIFO inventory accounting, fossil-fuel preferences and full first-year deduction of advertising. Spending caps now grow at inflation minus 1.5%%. Energy-bill credits and other income-tested benefits now phase out gradually instead of stopping at a fixed income line.

November 7, 2026

Open-source re-score: $5.0 trillion in ten-year deficit reduction

Superseded: the current estimate is $2.9 trillion (see the update above). Every tax provision was re-checked with the open-source Tax-Calculator model and IRS income statistics, and the Social Security changes against the Social Security actuaries' published estimates. Social Security tax now applies only to wages above $400,000. A new Section 3412 counts savings only once they are scored or audited.

November 7, 2026

One application for every federal benefit (Sec. 3907)

A single application screens a household for Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, SNAP and health coverage. Shared checks catch duplicate and mistaken payments, with privacy limits and a person reviewing every case. Each program keeps its own rules, and States choose whether to join.

November 7, 2026

Fiscal Sustainability Plan and American Resource Fund

Seven measures save about $305 billion a year at full effect (now $385 billion; see later updates), including spending caps, Medicare drug negotiation, Social Security solvency for future retirees and crop-insurance reform. New oil, gas and offshore-wind royalties go into a permanent fund.

November 7, 2026

The whole bill, searchable on one page

All 41 titles and 279 sections can now be searched and linked section by section, and the homepage shows the complete legislative and fiscal architecture.

November 6, 2026

2026 midterm results: what the new Congress can pass in 2027

House, Senate and governor results, every flipped seat, and the six NSRA planks with support from both parties that the 120th Congress can move first.
