NSRA Title XXXIX · Digital Government Modernization

Government That Works Online: Retire the Old Systems, Build One Front Door

The United States ranked first in the UN's e-government index in 2003 through 2005. In 2024 it ranked 19th. Benefit and payment systems built decades ago still run core programs, crash during emergencies, and leak money. Title XXXIX replaces them on a deadline and pays for it from the waste it stops.

What it does

ProvisionDraft text
Find the old systemsEvery major agency inventories mission-critical systems over 20 years old or on unsupported code within 180 days (Sec. 3902(a))
Retire them on a deadlineSeven-year retirement schedule, Social Security, Medicare, Medicaid, veterans', tax, and unemployment payments first (Sec. 3902(b))
Self-repaying fundA Legacy Retirement Account in the Technology Modernization Fund; agencies repay from documented savings (Sec. 3902(c))
Unemployment systems that holdGrants for State systems that handle ten times normal claim volume with built-in fraud detection (Sec. 3902(d))
One front doorOne secure portal to apply for and manage federal services; with your consent, tell the government once instead of re-entering the same information (Sec. 3903)
Privacy firstMinimum necessary data, Privacy Act protections, no data sales, no law-enforcement use without a warrant, and in-person and phone options (Sec. 3903(c)–(d))
Cost$5 billion a year for five years, paid first from improper-payment savings (Sec. 3905)

Operative text: Title XXXIX (Secs. 3901–3906).

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does old government software matter?

Outdated systems are expensive to maintain, hard to secure, and fail under surges, as many State unemployment systems did in 2020. They also make it harder to verify eligibility, which drives improper payments.

Will this create a national ID or a central database?

No. The portal uses the existing Login.gov service, shares information between agencies only with the individual's consent, limits data to the minimum necessary, bans data sales, and bars law-enforcement use without a warrant.

How is it paid for?

First from documented improper-payment savings, then, if needed, from capped deficit-neutral SIRF capacity. Agencies repay modernization awards from operating savings.

What happens to people who don't use the internet?

Section 3903(c) requires in-person and telephone alternatives for anyone who cannot or chooses not to use online services.
