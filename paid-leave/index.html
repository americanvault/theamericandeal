NSRA Title XXXVIII · Family & Workforce Security

Twelve Weeks of Paid Leave, More Child Care, and the Tradespeople America Needs

The United States is the only OECD country without a national paid leave entitlement for new parents. Thirteen States and D.C. already run paid leave insurance that works. Title XXXVIII brings that model nationwide, financed by its own small contribution rather than new deficit spending, and pairs it with child care supply and an apprenticeship credit for the jobs this bill creates.

What it does

ProvisionDraft text
12 weeks paid leaveFor a new child, a serious illness, caring for a sick family member, or a military deployment, matching the FMLA reasons (Sec. 3802(b))
Benefit66% of wages, 85% for low earners, up to a weekly cap of $1,000–$1,500 (Sec. 3802(d))
How it's paid for0.2% from workers and 0.2% from employers into a dedicated trust fund, with no employer share for businesses under 10 employees; actuarial solvency certified every three years (Sec. 3802(e))
Respects States and employersStates with equal or better programs keep them; employers with equal or better plans get a contribution credit (Sec. 3802(g)–(h))
Child care supplyGrants to States that add licensed slots, raise provider pay, and streamline licensing for home-based and faith-based providers; employer child care credit raised to $500,000 (Sec. 3803)
ApprenticeshipsA $2,500 credit per registered apprentice ($3,500 for veterans, under-25s, or manufacturing zones), refundable for small employers, prioritizing electricians, lineworkers, welders, and technicians (Sec. 3804)

Designed to win votes on both sides

Paid leave is self-financed through insurance, not general revenue. Small businesses pay no employer share. Faith-based and home-based child care compete on equal terms. The apprenticeship credit rewards employers who train workers instead of a new federal program. Child care and apprenticeship costs are capped at $16 billion a year from deficit-neutral SIRF capacity.

Operative text: Title XXXVIII (Secs. 3801–3806).

Frequently Asked Questions

How much would paid leave cost a worker?

The contribution is 0.2% of wages for the worker, about $2 on every $1,000 earned, and 0.2% for the employer. Businesses with fewer than 10 employees pay no employer share.

What if my State already has paid leave?

States with programs at least as generous may keep them, and workers and employers covered by them are credited for their State contributions.

Does it add to the deficit?

Paid leave is self-financed through its own trust fund. Child care and apprenticeship spending is capped at $16 billion a year and must be certified deficit-neutral by OMB.

Why apprenticeships?

The bill's grid, hydropower, nuclear, and manufacturing investments will need large numbers of electricians, lineworkers, welders, pipefitters, and technicians. The credit pays employers to train them.
