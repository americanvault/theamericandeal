The National Security & Resilience Act

Corporate Housing Reform, The 50-Unit Cap

The NSRA restores single-family housing to owner-occupants by capping any institutional investor at 50 single-family homes per Metropolitan Statistical Area, nationwide.

The 50-unit MSA cap

Title XIV limits any private-equity firm, REIT, or institutional entity to 50 single-family homes per metro area. It does not touch commercial or multi-family housing. Holdings above the cap trigger a mandatory, phased divestment.

Protecting the market during divestment

A volume-absorption filter limits forced sales to no more than 4.5% of local volume per quarter, preventing a fire-sale crash. Proceeds capitalize a Shelter Security Fund providing down-payment assistance to first-time buyers, expanding the owner-occupant buyer pool.

Built to survive a takings challenge

Divestiture happens through arms-length sales at fair market value. The government does not set prices or choose buyers. Owners get an independent-appraisal right, and no forced sale may close more than 10% below the certified appraisal. Owners keep the right to bring a takings claim in the Court of Federal Claims.

Codifying the 2026 executive order

On January 20, 2026, the President directed federal agencies to curb purchases of single-family homes by large institutional investors and called on Congress to put that policy into law. Title XIV is that law. It adds the divestiture, compensation, and enforcement framework that an executive order cannot supply.

Build more homes, not just fewer investor purchases

Capping institutional buyers frees up existing homes. Lowering prices for good also takes more homes. Section 1403 rewards cities and States that legalize duplexes, triplexes, and accessory units, allow apartments near transit, drop parking and lot-size minimums, decide code-compliant permits within 120 days, or allow office-to-residential conversions. Reforming jurisdictions get a scoring bonus in federal housing and transportation grants and Build More Homes Bonus Grants tied to the homes they actually permit. It is not a mandate: no community loses any funding for declining. Section 1403 also offers surplus federal land for housing and removes the permanent-chassis rule that holds back factory-built homes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the NSRA stop corporations from owning any homes?

No. It caps institutional owners at 50 single-family homes per metro area and does not affect commercial or multi-family property.

Will forced divestment crash home prices?

A volume-absorption throttle limits sales to 4.5% of local volume per quarter, spreading divestment out to protect local markets.
